# Customer journeys: how a visit actually goes

**Read this first.** These are drafts for you to mark up, not scripts. Nothing
here is built. I wrote them to find out what a real visit needs from the
system, and the last section lists what they imply.

How to read a transcript:

- `[walks to ...]` is a movement. `[state: ...]` is the visitor's stored state.
- **→C** or **→S** says who starts that exchange (customer or staff). Either can,
  always (your rule from 2026-10-12).
- Prices are placeholders. Amounts the customer says out loud are the customer's words.
- The satire is in the *lived* experience (T2, T6), the calm is in the
  *intended* one (T1, T3). The store should be able to produce both from the
  same machinery depending on staffing, stock and who you get.

Cast (invented): **Mira** (greeter), **Dan** (phones specialist), **Jo**
(audio specialist), **Tom** (laptops specialist), **Priya** (stock), **Sam** (genius at the bar), **Lee**
(technician). Customers are named per transcript.

## T1. The good one: upgrading, with a budget and a trade-in

*Customer: Aisha. Has a four-year-old phone with a bad battery. Wants to spend
about $1,100. Takes photos of her kid, runs out of storage.*

**→S** *[Aisha stops just inside the glass, looking at the tables. Mira, the greeter, who has been watching the door, walks over and stops about 2 m away.]*

**Mira:** Hi, welcome in. Is there something in particular you're after today, or are you just having a look?

**Aisha:** I think I need a new phone. Mine's dying by lunchtime.

**Mira:** That's no fun. Phones are on the second table on your left, and Dan's there. Do you want me to walk you over and introduce you?

**Aisha:** Yes please. *[state: browsing → waiting_for_help (needs phones)]*

*[Mira walks Aisha to the phones table. Dan finishes with someone else and turns to them. Mira says Aisha's name and "battery's dying" to Dan, and leaves.]*
*[state: in_conversation, with Dan]*

**Dan:** Hi Aisha, I'm Dan. So the battery's the problem. What are you using at the moment?

**Aisha:** It's the one two models back. Plain one, not the pro.

**Dan:** Okay. And what do you use it for most? Photos, games, work, mostly texting?

**Aisha:** Photos, mainly. Lots of video of my daughter. And I'm always out of space.

**Dan:** That tells me storage matters more to you than anything else on the spec sheet. How much is it showing now, roughly?

**Aisha:** It's 128, and it's full. I've deleted everything I can.

**Dan:** Right, so I'd say don't go back to 128. Have you got a number in mind for budget? No wrong answer, I'll just not show you things you'd hate the price of.

**Aisha:** About eleven hundred. I'd rather not go over.

**Dan:** That works. Two options. *[picks up two devices]* The standard is lighter and the battery will beat your current one by a long way. The pro has the better camera and zoom, and it's heavier. At 256 the standard is [price], at 256 the pro is [price], which is just over where you want to be.

**Aisha:** Is the camera really that different for video of a toddler?

**Dan:** For a toddler in a living room, honestly, not much. Where you'd see it is in low light and zoom. If you mostly shoot in daylight at home, the standard will do you well.

**Aisha:** Then the standard, 256.

**Dan:** Good call. Colours: blue, black, white, sage on the table now. *[hands her the blue one]* Hold that. See if the size is okay for you.

**Aisha:** *[holds it, a bit longer than polite]* I like the blue.

**Dan:** Great. Two things before I get one from the back. Do you want to trade your current phone in? It comes off the price. And do you want a case, or do you already have one?

**Aisha:** What's the trade-in?

**Dan:** I'd need to see it. If it turns on and the screen isn't cracked it's usually worth something. *[verify: how trade-in values are decided]* I can take a look right now, on my device.

**Aisha:** *[takes out her phone]* The back's cracked.

**Dan:** *[checks]* That brings the value down, but it still counts: [amount] off. So you'd be at [total]. Want to go with that?

**Aisha:** Yes. And a case, I suppose, since it's a new phone.

**Dan:** The clear ones show the colour. Cases are on the back wall; Jo will point you. Or I can bring one over with the phone. Which would you rather?

**Aisha:** Bring it.

**Dan:** Right. *[radios Priya in the back]* Give me a minute. Take a seat at the table if you like.
*[state: waiting_for_order. Aisha sits at the service table. Dan taps a request; Priya fetches blue / 256 / standard from the back. About a minute and a half passes. A clear case is brought out too.]*

**Priya:** *[arrives, hands Dan the box]* Blue, 256.

**Dan:** Thanks. *[to Aisha]* Okay, it's here. Do you want to pay now? I can do it here, you don't need to go to a till.

**Aisha:** *[taps card on Dan's device]* That was easy.

**Dan:** One more: do you want me to set the phone up with you now? We can move your stuff across while you sit here, about 20 minutes. Or you can do it at home.

**Aisha:** Now. I don't trust myself at home.

**Dan:** I'll start it, then you sign in to your account on it, and I'll keep an eye on it. [verify: store set-up service] *[state: in_purchase → in_setup]*

*[Aisha and Dan sit for a while. Dan answers two small questions (how to keep the old photos, whether the case fits). The old phone goes to Dan for trade-in.]*

**Dan:** Done. Everything's across. Your old one's on its way to being recycled, and the receipt is in your email. *[hands her the bag]* Enjoy it. If the battery isn't better in a fortnight, just come back.

**Aisha:** Thank you, genuinely.
*[state: leaving]*

What this transcript needs: greeter initiates; escort; a handover that carries
"battery's dying" and the need; budget, storage, current phone and use all
asked in a natural order; a trade-in check; a runner; payment by the
employee's own device; an optional set-up session.

## T2. The lived one: "just tell me where to tap" (after the reel)

*Customer: Derek. Wants a laptop. Has his card. In a hurry; the mall closes in 40 minutes.*

**→C** *[Derek walks straight to the first person in a blue shirt, Mira.]*

**Derek:** Hi, I want to buy a laptop. Where do I pay?

**Mira:** Of course! Do you have an appointment?

**Derek:** No. I just want to buy one. I've got the card.

**Mira:** No problem, I'll just check you in. Can I get your name?

**Derek:** Derek.

**Mira:** *[typing]* D-E-R-R-I-C-K?

**Derek:** One R. D-E-R-E-K.

**Mira:** Lovely. And what are we looking for today, Derek?

**Derek:** A laptop. The thin one. I don't care which, whichever's in stock.

**Mira:** The laptop team will be with you soon. Just pop yourself over by the laptop table and someone will find you.
*[state: waiting_for_help (needs laptops). Derek stands at the laptop table. Four minutes pass. Nobody comes. He starts testing a keyboard.]*

*[Dan, who is a phones specialist and not the laptops one, walks past and looks at Derek.]*

**→C Derek:** Excuse me, are you the laptop person?

**Dan:** I'm phones, sorry mate. But let me see who's on laptops. *[looks round]* Priya, are you on laptops?

**Priya:** *[from across the floor]* Stock! I'll find somebody.

*[A third staff member arrives. This is the laptops specialist, Tom.]*

**Tom:** Derek? Hi. You're after a laptop. Can I ask what you'd use it for?

**Derek:** Doesn't matter. Which is the one that's in stock?

**Tom:** Let me just check. *[taps device]* Which size did you want, the 13 or the 15?

**Derek:** The one on the table.

**Tom:** That's the 15. It's a good one, I'll just need to see who's available to bring it out. It might be a couple of minutes.

**Derek:** I have a card. Where do I tap?

**Tom:** We'll get you sorted when it's here. *[radios]* ... Sorry. We don't have that one in stock in that colour.

**Derek:** What colours do you have?

**Tom:** Only the silver, and that's the 13.

**Derek:** The 13 is fine. I just want to go.

**Tom:** Well, we can order the 15 and ship it to you. Or, I could offer you the 13 in silver. Do you want to look at that?

**Derek:** Yes. The 13. Silver. Now, please.

**Tom:** Right. I'll check we have it. *[taps]* It's at the back. I'll need someone to bring it out.
*[state: waiting_for_order. Another six minutes.]*

*[The 13 arrives. Tom takes payment on the device. The terminal asks Derek to tap. He taps. The screen spins.]*

**Tom:** Hmm. *[to himself]* That's not gone through. *[to Derek]* Sorry, can you try again?

*[Second tap: the same.]*

**Tom:** Let me get Sam from the bar. He handles payment problems. Don't go anywhere.

*[Derek watches Sam go to the bar and talk to a customer. Sam does not look up. The mall will close in 15 minutes.]*

What this transcript needs: the greeter cannot sell and says so; wrong
specialist and a handoff that restarts the wait; stock check that fails after
the customer chose; a fix-it offer (ship it, or take the 13) that costs
the customer something; payment as a thing that can stall.

## T3. A parent, a budget and an honest "I don't know"

*Customer: Greg, buying a first phone for his 11-year-old, Noah (who is
with him, bored). Wants to spend under $500. Greg isn't technical.*

**→S** *[Dan sees Greg turning over a box and walks up.]*

**Dan:** Hi, anything I can help find?

**Greg:** Is this a good phone for a kid?

**Dan:** It depends a bit on what he'll do with it. How old is he?

**Greg:** Eleven. Noah. He's going to the high school next year and he'll be on the bus.

**Dan:** Okay, so mostly calls and messages home, a bit of music, probably games. Is that fair?

**Greg:** Yes. I don't want him on the internet all day.

**Dan:** There's a feature for that: you set limits from your own phone, which apps, how long, and who he can contact. [verify: parental controls exist as described] Do you have a phone yourself? Which one?

**Greg:** The newer one. *[hands it over]*

**Dan:** Great, that works for family set-up. Now, about price. Under $500, you said. The newest ones start above that. The older generation is [price] new, same screen quality, slightly slower camera. For an eleven-year-old, I'd go there.

**Noah:** *[to Dan]* Does it do the camera thing where it's like, the portrait thing?

**Dan:** The older one does portrait mode, yes.

**Noah:** Is it the same as the pink one?

**Dan:** The pink one is the new one. It's [price]. *[to Greg]* I'd skip it.

**Greg:** What storage?

**Dan:** 128 is plenty for a kid who streams and doesn't take thousands of videos. If you want to be safe, 256 is [price] more. I'd go 128, and put him on the photo cloud plan if he runs out.

**Greg:** And a care plan? He's going to drop it.

**Dan:** Yes. For a kid, I'd say get it. It covers accidental damage with a fee per incident. [verify: care plan terms] For a phone this age the plan is [price] a month.

**Greg:** Is it worth it?

**Dan:** If he drops it once, probably. If he never does, no. I'd honestly say it's worth it for an eleven-year-old and a bus.

**Greg:** And can I get a call-and-text only plan? From the carrier. My plan is with [carrier].

**Dan:** I don't know that one. I don't want to guess and have you sign up for the wrong thing. Give me a sec. *[walks to the next specialist, asks, comes back after 40 seconds]* They say it's something you'd need to sort with your carrier, and that this phone works with any. I'm sorry I couldn't be more definite.

**Greg:** That's fine. Thanks for checking.

**Dan:** Let me get you a black one in 128 and a case. He'll want a colourful case. Cases are on the back wall, Noah, help yourself, but keep it under [limit].
*[Noah goes to the case wall. A staff member (Jo) asks him whether he needs help. He says no.]*

(The rest follows T1: runner, payment on Dan's device, optional set-up.)

What this transcript needs: **two** customers at once (the parent decides, the
child asks); a budget as a hard ceiling that removes options; a recommendation
that is *cheaper* than the upsell; the specialist **not knowing** and
checking (after Account 2, where the check came a few minutes later and
the answer was "actually no"); a lane the specialist doesn't own (carrier
plans) and says so.

## T4. The Genius Bar: a battery, a booking and a wait

*Customer: Hamza. Phone shuts down at 30%. Out of warranty, 2.5 years old.
Found out the bar takes walk-ins only if there's a free slot.*

**→C** *[Hamza walks in and goes to Mira at the door.]*

**Hamza:** My phone keeps switching off. I think it's the battery. Do I need an appointment?

**Mira:** It's best, yes. The bar books in slots (every 20 minutes); the next free one is 3:40. Is that okay?

**Hamza:** I can do 3:40. It's 3:05.

**Mira:** I'll put you in. Your name? *[checks him in]* You're in the queue. You can have a look round, or sit at the bar. We'll call your name.
*[state: waiting_for_help (needs genius). Hamza wanders. At the audio wall Jo says hi; he says he's just looking.]*

*[35 minutes pass in the store. At 3:40 Sam calls "Hamza?" and gestures to a stool at the bar.]*

**→S Sam:** Hamza? Come on over. I'm Sam. Tell me what's going on.

**Hamza:** It switches off at 30%. Sometimes it goes to 40.

**Sam:** Does it happen when it's cold?

**Hamza:** Maybe. It was cold this morning.

**Sam:** Okay, can I run a diagnostic? Takes about two minutes. *[plugs it in]* Take a seat.
*[Hamza watches. A progress bar. Sam taps a few things.]*

**Sam:** So the battery health reads 71%. That's below the threshold where we'd say it's performing as it should. [verify: threshold and phrase] The shutdowns match.

**Hamza:** Is it covered?

**Sam:** It's out of warranty, so it's a paid repair. [price]. It takes about an hour and a half here, or I can have the technician do it now if there's a bench free. *[checks]* There's one.

**Hamza:** Is there any risk to my data?

**Sam:** It's a battery swap, so the phone isn't wiped. I'd still say back it up if you can. Do you have a recent one?

**Hamza:** Last night.

**Sam:** Good. So: repair today, [price], 90 minutes. Or do nothing and I can tell you what to turn off. Which?

**Hamza:** Repair. Can I wait?

**Sam:** Yes. Technician Lee will take it. Here's a ticket. You can leave the store and come back, or wait at the bar.
*[state: in_purchase (repair), waiting_for_order. Hamza leaves for lunch. He returns; the phone is at the pickup.]*

**Lee:** *[hands it over]* New battery, 100%. Tested. Your old one was swollen a bit, just so you know. Good thing it's out.

**Hamza:** Thanks. Do I pay here?

**Lee:** Sam can take it. *[points]*

What this transcript needs: **appointment** and **queue** as visible shared
state (this is the thing the course marks); a diagnosis that reads the
device and gives a real reason; an out-of-warranty price decision; a
**repair ticket** that outlives the visit; and "Sam can take payment",
a handoff at the end. "Go to the Genius Bar" has to *produce a queue entry*
and a place for the human genius to see it, not just a sentence.

## T6. The payment limbo (after Account 1)

*Customer: Rin. Buying two phones. Took 30 minutes to decide, 10 minutes for
the phones to come out. Taps to pay. The employee vanishes.*

*[Dan, with Rin, says: "One sec, I'll just process this at the back" and leaves with the card-reader device. 4 minutes pass.]*

**→C Rin:** *[to the nearest staff member, Jo]* Excuse me. The person who was helping me has gone.

**Jo:** Who was it?

**Rin:** Dan. Blue shirt. He took my payment.

**Jo:** I don't know who you've spoken to so far. There are a lot of us on today. Can you wait over where you were told to?

**Rin:** I've been waiting 15 minutes.

**Jo:** Sorry. I'll try to find out.
*[Jo looks around, can't see Dan, asks Mira at the door, who doesn't know either.]*

*[After another 10 minutes, Sam comes over from the bar.]*

**Sam:** Rin? I'm sorry. The payment didn't go through on our side.

**Rin:** It says paid on my banking app.

**Sam:** The money's with the bank but not with us. It's in limbo. It happens.

**Rin:** So what happens now?

**Sam:** There's not much I can do from here. Your bank has to contact us.

**Rin:** I have spent two hours here. What do you suggest?

**Sam:** I can put the phones aside for you for 24 hours and ask the team to chase it. Or you could ask for a refund and buy online.

*[Options for Rin: wait for the bank (phones held), refund and leave, ask for a manager, ask who was the staff member and complain.]*

What this transcript needs: **payment as a state with an outcome nobody in
the room can see** (D-scope's "outcome no dialogue option fixes"), a
**handoff that loses context** (Jo asks "who was it?"), and a human employee
who can *choose* to be helpful or dismissive. Don't make this the normal
path. It should be rare, and it should be the thing a good employee can
rescue.

## What these transcripts imply

### The structure of a conversation

Every real exchange in these seven has the same skeleton. The current
scripts do not:

1. **Greet.** Either side. (T1, T3, T4 start from staff; T2, T4, T5 from the customer.)
2. **Find the need.** Open: what are you after, what for.
3. **Discover facts.** A small set of questions, asked in the order that fits
   the answer: what they have now, what they use it for, budget, storage,
   colour, trade-in, accessories, care plan, payment.
4. **Recommend and compare.** Two options with a trade-off, not a list.
5. **Fetch.** A runner. A visible wait.
6. **Close.** Pay on the employee's device. Optional set-up.
7. **Handoff** can happen between any two steps, carrying a note.

### What a customer carries (the state to store)

Budget (a ceiling, maybe none), what they own now, what they use it for,
storage need, colour, trade-in (yes/no, condition), accessories wanted,
care plan (yes/no), who is deciding (self, parent), what has been
**promised** to them, a **basket**, and an **order/ticket** that outlives
the visit (T4, T5).

### Employees

- Each employee reply is a **menu** like the customer's (your rule), with
  the exact form: a line spoken, then numbered choices. Free text is never
  needed.
- An employee must be able to **see** what the customer has told the
  previous employee (note on the handoff, basket, ticket), or T6 happens.
- An employee must be able to **walk** the customer somewhere and **leave**
  them with the next person (T1, T7), not "wait over there".
- Only someone **near** the customer can start or take a conversation. A
  panel that claims across the room breaks the whole "find them, greet
  them" idea.

### Places

Speciality tables (phones, laptops, tablets, audio), the case wall, the
charging wall, the Genius Bar with a **queue and slots** (T4), the **service
tables** for sitting down (T1, T3), a **pickup table** (T5), the back room
with **stock**.

### What T2 and T6 do that the others don't

They are the intended-versus-lived gap. They aren't "bad staff" (the people
are trying). They come from **narrow roles** (greeter can't sell),
**invisible state** (nobody can see payment), and **no way to find a
colleague**. The same machinery as T1 with a different staffing level should
produce them.

## What I'd settle with you before building anything

These are your calls; none of them has a default I'd pick quietly.

1. **Which of the seven to build first.** T1 plus T4 together cover
   greeter → specialist → runner → pay and the Genius queue; T2, T6 are the
   same machinery failing.
2. **How deep a conversation goes.** T1 is about 14 exchanges. Is that the
   target, or is a shorter version the crit-time version?
3. **Do prices and models exist at all?** The transcripts assume a few
   phones with real-feeling choices (storage, colour, a standard and a pro, an
   older generation). The stock table has three placeholder phones now.
4. **Trade-in, care plan and set-up:** in or out for the first build?
5. **Basket and ticket persistence:** database rows (as the player state is)
   or per-conversation only?
6. **Does an NPC specialist run the same transcripts as a human one?** My
   suggestion is yes: one script, two drivers, so a visitor can't tell
   which they got until they ask a hard question.
