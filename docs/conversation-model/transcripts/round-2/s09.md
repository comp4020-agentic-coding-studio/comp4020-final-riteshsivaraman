Customer throw-backs used:
- budget_is_firm (canned, NEW): tells staff the ceiling will not move. Effect: marks budget as firm, so staff should not re-ask it. The nudge still happens once.
- Parameterised pick "Set my own ceiling: A$1,800" at C4.

## What staff could have asked but didn't

- offer_demo at C3 (pool was 2): skipped. With a firm, impatient customer a demo of the 18 Pro before knowing the budget would waste his time, and the budget question was the only one that could split the pool further.
- hardware_features, screen_size_pref, storage_need: the pool was 2 models and the next answer decided it.
- Reopening the budget ("is A$1,800 really firm?"): redundant after the canned throw-back.
- Asking about colour: no point with an empty pool.

## Assumptions I made

1. Staff do not push a second relax nudge after "Keep both". They move straight to rescue_offer, then close. A staff who nudged twice would risk more impatience; the engine can decide.
2. S5 lists the 16, 17e and 17 as models under A$1,800 without the separate lens. That is derived from price (A$1,349, A$1,149, A$1,549); the Air (A$1,899) is over.
3. "2x from the main camera" is the wording for the optical-quality 2x crop, which every model's main camera lines advertise. No model without a "Fusion Telephoto" line is described as having a separate telephoto lens.
4. Staff did not say anything about the customer's A$1,800 limit being negotiable. The "I can't change the price" line follows the scenarios file note that staff have no pricing authority in this scene.
5. rescue_offer reuses the round 1 name. Its options here are the 17 (nearest on price, 2x crop only) and the 18 Pro (nearest on the telephoto requirement).
6. Same budget design as s04 and s06: a parameterised "Set my own ceiling" pick so A$1,800 can be expressed.
7. S6 uses move=other because no listed move fits an offer after a declined nudge; a rescue_offer may deserve its own move label.
