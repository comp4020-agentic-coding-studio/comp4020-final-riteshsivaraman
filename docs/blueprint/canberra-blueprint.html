What the references say, and what changed

From the corners you set in the rectify tool (docs/blueprint/rectify.json) the references gave a shop about 10 m wide by 10 m deep under a 3 m wall (side walls 3.35 times as long as high, back wall 3.3 times as wide as high). You then asked for more room so the tables are spread out and people can move between them, and chose to keep the 3 m wall. The shop is now 11 m wide by 15.4 m deep by 3 m high, so it is deliberately wider and much longer than the reference proportions.

Where the scale is uncertain

  • The 3 m wall height is the one absolute number. The photographs fix the proportions; everything in metres follows from it. If the real ceiling is higher, the whole shop scales with it.
  • Table length, the entrance opening and the back room are assumptions.
  • Wall features (lightboxes, bays, monitors) are placed by their proportion of the wall in the close-up photographs.

Tables: position and orientation

Three columns across (the left column, the centre column and the right column as you enter). The left and right columns each have four almost-square product tables and, nearest the bar, a larger square service table with two stools on its left and two on its right, tucked under the table, where a customer can sit and talk with staff. The centre column has three product tables and one square service table. Product tables carry a product on each of their four sides, facing outward; service tables carry nothing.
